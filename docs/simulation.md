# Simulated data

No sensor recordings are published. All test data in this repository is
generated by the two simulators below.

## 1. Sensor simulator (`src/sensor_sim.py`)

Renders a scene into `(N, 8, 8)` int16 frames that look like the SEN0628's
output.

**Scene model.** Units are mm, in camera view with the origin on the optical
axis. A scene is a wall at distance `wall`, with optional tilt
(`tilt_x`, `tilt_y` in mm per mm), plus a list of `Box(x, y, w, h, depth)`
objects. Each box is a rectangular face of size `w x h` centred at `(x, y)`,
standing `depth` mm off the wall toward the sensor. A negative `depth` models
a recess.

**Ray model.** The 60° square field is split into 8x8 zones, and each zone is
sampled with 12x12 sub-rays. Each sub-ray takes the perpendicular distance of
the nearest surface it hits.

**Per-frame measurement model**, matched to bench characterisation:

| Effect | Model |
|---|---|
| Solid-surface noise | Gaussian, σ = 5 mm (8 mm under 200 mm) |
| Mixed zones (two surfaces, > 15 mm apart) | mean of the sub-rays, plus extra jitter up to 20 mm scaled by `4f(1−f)`, where `f` is the fraction of the zone covered by the nearer surface |
| Flicker | in mixed zones, 15% of frames snap to the near surface and 15% to the far surface |
| Dropouts | mixed zones drop to `4000` with a per-zone probability drawn from 0–90%, scaled by mixedness |
| Limits | readings below 20 mm or at/above 4000 become `4000` |

Run `python3 sensor_sim.py` to print the mean and std of an example scene.
The std map shows the straddling ring of zones around the box.

## 2. Shape scenes (`src/shapes.py`)

`shapes.scene(mask, wall, depth, crop, jitter)` turns any 5x5 boolean mask
into boxes. Each raised cell becomes a box exactly one zone wide at the face
distance, placed at the chosen 8x8 crop offset. `jitter` shifts the whole
shape by a random fraction of a zone, to test misalignment.

## 3. Test suites

`src/sweep.py` generates 52 scenes and runs each through the full pipeline
(`grid.frames_to_h(auto, binary)` → `run_sim.run` → `detect.detect`):

- squares of 2, 3 and 4 zones, with walls at 250, 300 and 400 mm and five
  sub-zone offsets
- rectangles of 3x4, 4x3 and 2x3 zones
- a recessed square
- controls: a bare wall, a tilted wall, a 2 cm step (rejected by design), a
  1-zone object (rejected by design), and two separate blocks

`src/shape_test.py` runs all 17 library shapes, aligned and at 0.3-zone
misalignment, through the pipeline and `identify.identify`. Seeds are derived
deterministically from the shape name.

## 4. Analytic encoding simulation (`sim/square5x5.py`)

Because the state is a product state, each Bell pair can be sampled exactly
from its closed-form outcome distribution, without a quantum SDK:

```
P(00) = cos²((θa−θb)/2)/2    P(01) = sin²((θa+θb)/2)/2
P(10) = cos²((θa+θb)/2)/2    P(11) = sin²((θa−θb)/2)/2
```

It adds the same readout and CNOT noise and scores eight idealised scenes at
1000 and 100 shots.

### Step size compared with detectability (100 shots, noise floor ≈ 0.012)

| Step as a fraction of the depth window | Boundary P(11) |
|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0.012 (buried) |
| 0.16 | 0.03 (marginal) |
| 0.30 | 0.10 (clear) |
| 0.60 | 0.33 |

This is why the pipeline snaps to near/far, or normalises to a tight window
around the scene, before encoding.
